The project setting and the results come from the broader framework treated in [1].

We consider the problem of maximizing an investor’s asymptotic growth rate under model
uncertainty, in the presence of stability, that is ergodic dynamics for the market. In particular,
we study

sup_{θ∈Θ} inf_{P∈Π} g(θ; P)

where Θ is the set of admissible trading strategies, Π is the set of admissible measures and
g(θ; P) is the investor’s asymptotic growth rate when they adopt the trading strategy θ and the
dynamics of the market are governed by P.

Let us fix an integer d ≥ 1 and a non-empty set E ⊂ R^d. The market consists of d risky
assets with discounted prices X = (X1, · · · , Xd) taking values in E.

Consider the canonical space (Ω, F, F), where Ω is the set of all E-valued continuous trajectories
with the topology of locally uniform convergence, F is the Borel σ-algebra and F = (Ft)t≥0
is the right-continuous filtration generated by X.

For simplicity here, let us assume that (Xt)t≥0 is a d-dimensional Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process.
As input, we give ourselves two symmetric positive definite matrices c, Σ ∈ S_d++ and a
function p : E → (0, ∞) given by

p(x) = 1 / ((2π)^(d/2) det(Σ)^(1/2)) * exp(-(1/2) x^⊤ Σ^(-1) x)

that, you might recognize, is the density of an N(0, Σ) distribution. The matrix c serves as the
instantaneous covariance of the process X.

Let us assume that X has ergodic density p. Define the wealth process at time T > 0 for
a strategy θ ∈ Θ and with P ∈ Π governing the market dynamics as

V_T^θ := E(∫_0^T θ_t^⊤ dX_t)

where E is the stochastic exponential.

The asymptotic growth rate of the portfolio under the measure P with strategy θ is defined as

g(θ; P) := sup { γ ∈ R : lim inf_{T→∞} (1/T) log V_T^θ ≥ γ, P-a.s. }

You might here also recognize the log-utility problem presented in the lecture.

Let A(x) = cp(x) be the averaged covariance matrix and consider the classes

D = {
φ ∈ C^2(E) :
∫_E |Tr(A(x)∇²e^φ(x)) / e^φ(x)| dx < ∞
}

and

Θ₀ = {
θ ∈ Θ : θ_t = ∇φ(X_t), φ ∈ D
}

Finally, define the growth rate invariance property:
"g(θ, P) is independent of P ∈ Π for every θ ∈ Θ₀".

Part II.

For the second part of the project, the goal is to robustify via adversarial training to compute
the robust-optimal growth rate. Note that, in the general case, this is a non-trivial question.
However, in the case of a multi-dimensional Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process that we consider here,
it is more amenable.

Consider an Agent that trades in X and an Adversary, that we call "Nature", that decides the
drift of X. For the optimization to be robust, it must be performed over all dynamics that
induce the ergodic distribution p in the long run.

1. Write down the d-dimensional OU dynamics of X (in a general form). Which specific
parameters within the drift term will the Adversary perturb to induce the worst-case
scenario for the agent?

2. When modifying the drift, what constraint must Nature satisfy to maintain a valid stationary
distribution? Specifically, how is the Fokker–Planck equation used to relate the drift matrix
and the diffusion matrix to the stationary covariance matrix Σ?

3. In terms of d, write down the number of degrees of freedom the Fokker–Planck equation
allows. Justify your answer and argue.

4. Propose a design for the Agent and Adversary as neural networks and define the loss
functions for this minimax game. Specify the architectures and justify why these choices
are appropriate.

5. Formulate the training procedure to learn the robust-optimal strategy. How do you handle
the potential instabilities of this objective?

6. Plot the learned strategy and report the computed robust-optimal growth rate. What do
you observe?

7. Compare the analytical result of Part I. with the ML approach of Part II and comment
